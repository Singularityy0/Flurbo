Part 1 · Pros and cons

What each one gets right and wrong

Flurbo (today)

Pros
  • Only venue pricing combined bets from one pool, so prices always agree
  • Four general readers settle most public-data questions with no new code
  • Pre-test-v1 settled four questions from four sources with no operator input
  • Evidence anyone can replay; trading closes before every observation window
  • Liquidity provider's loss fixed in advance
  • Best agent tooling; passkey sign-up and starter gas
Cons
  • Only the operator creates markets
  • Logins, secret keys, JavaScript-only pages, PDFs and subjective questions still need the operator
  • Disputes go to a panel the operator controls on testnet
  • Operator carries the whole subsidy
  • No fees, no creator reward, no brand yet

The most transparent engine on Monad, still with a closed front door.

Levr

Pros
  • Leverage replaces parlays with one simple multiplier
  • Largest testnet user base on Monad
  • LP vault lets outsiders earn the house edge
  • Well funded
Cons
  • Sports only; closed listing
  • Prices copied from sportsbooks, so no new price discovery
  • Leverage means liquidations on normal odds swings
  • Results oracle and fees not publicly documented

A leveraged sportsbook more than a forecasting market.

Hyperstitions

Pros
  • Genuinely new idea: markets that fund action
  • Permissionless creation
  • Virtual liquidity allows markets that last hours
  • zkTLS proof of the API response before settlement
Cons
  • Manipulation is the product, so prices are not forecasts
  • Collateral is its own volatile token
  • An AI prompt still decides the outcome
  • Thin documentation; docs were down during research

A coordination tool with a market attached.

Blinq

Pros
  • Fully mechanical settlement from Pyth
  • Gasless smart accounts; very fast rounds
  • Simple products anyone understands
Cons
  • Price-direction games, not information markets
  • Predefined payouts imply a house edge
  • Volume boosted by airdrop points
  • No user-created markets

Closer to a trading game than a prediction market.

CRSHMARKET

Pros
  • A market category nobody else has
  • About 60 second settlement suits live viewing
  • Strong consumer pull and early volume
Cons
  • Resolution process not documented
  • Streamers can control outcomes; insider and fakery concerns in the press
  • Regulatory exposure of betting on people's lives
  • No evidence a user can check

Entertainment first; trust is the open question.

Polymarket

Pros
  • Deepest crypto liquidity and biggest audience
  • About 2h to resolve when undisputed
  • Careful rules text with on-chain clarifications
Cons
  • Users cannot create markets
  • Disputes decided by UMA token holders
  • Niche books are thin or empty

Wins popular questions on depth and brand.

Kalshi

Pros
  • Regulated in the US; dollars in and out
  • Precise contract terms and named sources
  • Class certification: one template, many markets
Cons
  • Only Kalshi lists markets
  • Settlement is trust in the company
  • RFQ combos have been priced worse than sportsbooks for takers

The regulated benchmark for rules quality.

Delphi

Pros
  • Anyone can create, with a guided wizard
  • AI validator catches vague questions
  • Creators earn 1.5% of volume
Cons
  • A model decides; no appeal documented
  • Receipts prove the model ran, not that it was right
  • Up to 48h to settle

Best creator experience, weakest settlement guarantee.

Seer

Pros
  • Permissionless, any question
  • Reality.eth bonds plus Kleros jurors
  • Scalar, multi-scalar and futarchy markets
Cons
  • Days to weeks to settle
  • Thin, per-outcome liquidity
  • Loose wording ends as "Invalid"

Most neutral settlement, slowest and thinnest markets.

bento.fun

Pros
  • Markets in seconds, public or invite-only
  • Built to spread through group chats
  • SDK for other apps
Cons
  • Resolution and pricing not documented
  • Mostly sports; small stakes by design

Best growth loop, least transparent settlement.

PART 2

Flurbo's shift to overall winner

Keep what only Flurbo has, borrow one proven idea from each rival, and fix the two problems that matter most: every market settled mechanically, and small creator losses.

Part 2 · Best of all

What to take from each

Keep from Flurbo

One pool, general readers, replayable evidence

Joint-state LMSR, the four general readers, the rule check and hashed captures are the moat. Nothing below should weaken them.

From Hyperstitions

zkTLS for sources nobody else can fetch

Reclaim-style proofs let a reader use logged-in or keyed APIs while anyone can still verify the response. Take the proof, leave out the AI judge.

From Blinq and CRSHMARKET

Short-horizon markets

Minutes-long markets keep people coming back. Flurbo can do this mechanically with evm-rpc.v1 on Monad blocks and on-chain price feeds.

From Levr

An LP vault for depth

Outside LPs fund the market maker and share fees, tranched so no single market can drain it. Here it funds LMSR depth instead of leverage.

From Kalshi

Templates as a class

Review a reader template once; every market made from it is pre-approved and only varies its parameters.

From Delphi

Guided creation and a creator cut

A wizard with live validation and a fee share. Flurbo's validator is the real rule check, not a model's opinion.

From Seer

Reality.eth and Kleros as the fallback

Questions no reader can check still get neutral settlement, labelled slow so traders price it in.

From Polymarket

Short bonded window, exact rules text

A 1 to 2 hour window is enough when evidence is mechanical, and each template generates Kalshi-grade rules text.

From bento.fun

Private duels and share links

Invite-only markets settled by a reader, so small stakes never depend on trusting the host.

The plan removes the operator from each of those.

Fallback · Reality.eth + Kleros

Transparency lesson from pre-test-v1. EMSC entered an M4.2 earthquake 92 minutes after it happened, five minutes after Flurbo captured the count. The market settled on 4 while the catalogue later showed 5. Flurbo was right by its committed rule, and the rule check now refuses any capture window less than 3 hours after an observed interval. Publishing this kind of detail is exactly what the other venues don't do.

Settlement path for every market

A new reader is one more adapter value

The rule format already carries the reader and its settings inside the hashed rule. A zkTLS reader keeps the same shape: the proof replaces the raw capture as evidence, and the extraction settings stay identical to json-api.v1.

A creator never writes free text for settlement. They pick a template, fill its settings, and the page shows the generated rules text and the rule check result before they can publish.

// flurbo.settlement-rule.v1, proposed zkTLS reader
{
  "source": {
    "publisher": "Official league stats API",
    "urls": ["https://api.example-league.com/match/8812"],
    "adapter": "zktls-json.v1",
    "reader": {
      "select": "/result/winner",
      "aggregate": "value",
      "checks": [{ "path": "/status", "equals": "final" }]
    },
    "proof": { "provider": "reclaim", "verify": "on-chain" }
  },
  "decision": { "type": "category", "equals": "HOME" },
  "onNoValidCapture": "reality-eth"
}

Three ways to bring in Seer, Reality.eth and Kleros

A. Reality.eth on Monad

Fast to ship

Deploy the open-source Reality.eth contracts on Monad. Flurbo's reviewer panel is the arbitrator at first.

  • Bonded public answers from day one
  • Arbitration is still the panel

B. Read Seer's answers

Recommended first

Mirror each fallback question to Reality.eth on Gnosis, where Seer and Kleros already run. Flurbo settles by reading the final answer with evm-rpc.v1 (metric: "call"), which already exists.

  • The fallback becomes just another mechanical source
  • Real Kleros jurors, no bridge, no new Flurbo contracts
  • Question bonds paid on Gnosis

C. Kleros cross-chain proxy

Most native

Kleros already links Reality.eth on other chains through home and foreign proxies over a message bridge. Monad would need a bridge and an audit.

  • Disputes start from Monad directly
  • Most engineering and review work

Result: most questions settle mechanically in about two hours. The rest settle through bonds and jurors in days, labelled "slow settlement". The operator no longer proposes or decides any outcome.

PROBLEM 2

Keeping creator losses small

An LMSR market maker can lose at most b·ln N, where b is the depth and N the number of joint outcomes. Pre-test-v1 already cut depth to b = 25, so four linked questions cost 69.3 test AUSD at worst. The worst case only happens if the market ends at full certainty after traders moved the price all the way. Six levers shrink it, and fees can pay it back.

Loss calculator

Worst-case subsidy–
Creator fee income–fees repay the subsidy first
Net, worst case–

Worst case assumes the market resolves at full certainty and the whole subsidy is paid out. With depth growth, b = b₀ + 0.05 × volume while worst-case loss stays capped at the starting depth, following Othman et al.'s liquidity-sensitive LMSR. Illustrative, not a quote.

The six levers, in the order to build them

1

Start shallow, deepen with volume

Liquidity-sensitive LMSR: b grows as money trades, so creators fund only a small starting b. Worst-case loss tends to zero as starting depth does.

Yes/no at b = 10: about 7 AUSD at risk

2

Fees repay the subsidy first

A 1 to 2% fee goes to the creator until the subsidy is covered, then splits with the protocol.

At 2%, pre-test-v1's 69.3 AUSD is repaid by about 3,470 AUSD of volume

3

An LP vault shares the risk

Levr-style: outside LPs fund depth for fees, tranched per market so no single market drains the vault.

Creator risk divided among backers

4

Close before the answer is knowable

Already in Flurbo: trading closes before every observation window and the rule check refuses already-public values. Add an early halt if a price passes 97%.

Removes the late-certainty tail of the worst case

5

Return what is left

After payouts the pool's remaining collateral returns to the creator. The creation screen shows worst-case and expected loss before publishing.

Creators see their exact maximum before they commit

6

Protocol matching for new creators

A treasury matches the first slice of subsidy for creators with a clean track record, to seed long-tail markets.

Turn on once fees exist

Part 2 · After the shift

Where Flurbo lands, and what it still gives up

With the plan in place, Flurbo leads the Monad field on trust, niche liquidity, combined bets and bad-market protection, and leads the global field on transparency. Flip the scorecard to "Flurbo with plan" to see it. These trade-offs remain:

Topic range still trails free text

Delphi, Seer and Hyperstitions accept any wording. Flurbo accepts any template. The fallback closes most of the gap at the cost of speed.

Slower than Blinq and CRSHMARKET

Their markets finish in a minute. Flurbo's bonded window takes about two hours, which is the cost of a result anyone can challenge.

Creators still post capital

Much less than today, but not zero like staff-listed venues.

Sports depth stays with Levr, Polymarket and Kalshi

Vaults and order books beat a subsidised pool on big games. Flurbo should not fight there.

Readers need upkeep

Sources change formats and add records late, as EMSC did. Adapters stay versioned, and a broken source drops to the fallback, never to a guess.

zkTLS is young

Proof providers differ in trust assumptions. Keep plain captures where a public source exists.

Positioning on Monad: Levr for sports, Blinq for quick price calls, CRSHMARKET for streams, Hyperstitions for coordination. Flurbo is the one where related questions price together and every result comes with evidence you can check yourself.
